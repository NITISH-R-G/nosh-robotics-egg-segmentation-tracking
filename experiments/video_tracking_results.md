## What happened (observed in the overlays; the figure `outputs/figures/video_tracking_trajectories.png` is generated from the tracks; footage stills are not published, see below)
- **Works:** stationary, un-occluded eggs, including blurred spinning eggs (blur did not stop the detector on the sampled frames). Detection coverage is 98% of expected eggs on sampled frames.
- **Fragmentation (T0):** when the hand carries Q about 1000 px to the right, the track is lost for ~9 steps and the gate (which grows linearly with missed steps) is too small to re-link; a new ID starts at the spin. T1's scene prior re-links it correctly (hypothesis: only one lost track existed at that moment).
- **Identity switch (both variants, a real failure):** at ~744-750 the hand picks up P and carries it right while Q sits at the far left. Both eggs are near each other and under the hand; the track of P (left at x~0.25) continues onto the stationary far-left egg (Q), and P re-appears later under a new ID. Verified visually in a 3-frame zoom of the footage (stills are kept locally, not published: third-party video). With two visually identical eggs and no appearance cue, a position/motion tracker cannot detect that the hand swapped which egg is where; a hand model or per-egg appearance (a mark on one egg) would be needed.
- **Track continuity through occlusion:** 1 of 3 occlusion gaps keeps its ID with T1, 0 of 3 with T0. This is 3 events, so it is an illustration, not a rate.
- **False tracks:** none observed (no ID created for a non-egg object); the extra IDs are fragments of real eggs.

## Runtime (this machine, CPU; relevant parameters in the protocol)
Detector median 8.3 ms/frame at 960 px width; tracker 0.1 ms/frame. AV1 1080p decoding is much slower than either and is not included; the full-rate video export decodes every frame.

## Limitations
- Tracking evaluated at 4 Hz on one 31-second clip with two eggs; 3 occlusion events; one annotator; the annotation was written after seeing all frames (held-out frames are not unseen, only untuned).
- The two-egg prior (T1) is an assumption of the scene, not a general tracker feature.
- The detector is specific to white eggs on a dark table; on the 110-image dataset it would not be a valid method (use S1 or the documented classical baseline there).
